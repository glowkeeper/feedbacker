Free and open source · For higher education

AI-assisted marking, feedback and moderation. You decide every mark.

Feedbacker helps educators mark and moderate coursework against a rubric and brief. The AI suggests levels and drafts feedback; you approve every mark and every word a student receives. It runs on your own computer.

See it in use, no install needed Get started

I am Steve Huckle, a university educator, and I use Feedbacker for real marking and moderation. In my first real moderation it saved me time without compromising my judgement: read how it went.

What it does

Marking, and moderation. Your judgement in both.

Marking a cohort, with feedback

  • Import every student's submission at once, from the bulk download of Turnitin or Canvas.
  • Mark each submission against the rubric. You choose whether to see the AI's suggested level for each criterion before you mark, or only after you have marked it (blind).
  • The AI drafts feedback from your marks and comments. Feedbacker then checks the draft against them, and flags, for example, praise the mark doesn't support, or a missing next step.
  • Compare the cohort's feedback side by side, approve exactly what each student receives, and paste or export it.

Moderation: checking a colleague's marking

  • Import the sample of marked work you were asked to check, with the original marker's marks and comments.
  • Mark each piece yourself against the rubric, with or without seeing the original marks first, and with the AI's own independent reading alongside.
  • See where you, the original marker and the AI differ, criterion by criterion, and record your verdict on each piece.
  • Approve and export the moderation record: a summary ready to copy into your institution's moderation form.

What it never does

AI assists.
Educators decide.

01

It never gives a mark

The AI suggests a level for each criterion, with its reasons and quotes from the work. Any provisional mark from those suggestions is calculated, kept apart from yours, and never becomes yours.

02

Nothing released without you

You approve exactly what each student receives, and any later change clears that approval. Feedbacker never sends anything to students or to your institution's systems.

03

Every AI contribution traceable

Each suggestion and draft records which AI made it, the instructions it was given and what it was sent, and feedback adapted from an AI draft is labelled as such.

04

Accessible by design

Every screen is measured against checks from WCAG 2.2 AA, the accessibility standard UK universities are expected to meet. Every control can be reached from the keyboard, and it has been used with a screen reader.

Responsible use

Careful with students' work. Honest about the limits.

01

Only what you approve leaves your computer

There is no Feedbacker server and no account. Nothing goes to the AI until you have reviewed it and confirmed it, and each request carries only the parts it needs. That is data minimisation by design.

02

Names replaced before anything is sent

Names, IDs, emails, links and phone numbers are replaced with tokens on your computer, and the key that links them back never leaves it. That is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation: the work is still personal data, and is treated as such.

03

A record of what was sent

A log on your computer records every request to the AI: when, which AI, a fingerprint of what was sent, and the cost. It holds no text and no names, so it can be shown to someone checking, without showing them students' work.

04

Honest about what it can miss

Redaction follows rules and can miss a name nobody listed, or an indirect identifier such as an employer. It can't see inside images, so you look at each one. Your review is the safeguard, and the limits are written down.

Feedbacker claims no certification and no compliance with any law: your institution makes its own assessment, with its data protection officer. There is a page written for them, with what is sent and when, the roles, the AI provider's terms to check, and a list of what to confirm before educators use it.

Your data

Nothing leaves your computer until you approve it.

Feedbacker is not a website you upload work to: there are no accounts and no Feedbacker server. It runs on your own computer, and your work lives in folders you keep.

Students' names, IDs and contact details in the text are replaced with tokens on your computer before anything can be sent (the app calls this step Anonymisation). Strictly, that is pseudonymisation: you hold the key that links the tokens back, so the work is still personal data and is handled as such. Images in the work, such as charts and photos, aren't redacted: you look at each one, and choose whether to send it. You approve each pseudonymised submission, with its images.

Only then, and only when you confirm it, is anything sent to the AI (Anthropic's Claude, with your own API key):

  • for its suggested levels: Feedbacker's instructions, the rubric, the pseudonymised submission and brief, and the images you approved;
  • to draft feedback: much the same, without the images, and with your own levels, marks and comments for that one student, and the guidance on feedback you approved;
  • to suggest an edit: the instructions and the rubric, that piece of your feedback with its flags, and your marking of that criterion; not the submission.

Never the names, IDs or original files, nor another student's work. It all goes through a small program on your computer that keeps a log of what it sends, without the text itself. Redaction follows rules, and can miss things: your review is the safeguard.

Where it stands

Used for real, and clear about its limits.

Marking and moderation are both built. I have used each for real, and written up how it went: read my accounts of marking and feedback and of moderation.

Not yet: an installer; marking in a team, and agreeing standards across markers; AI from providers other than Anthropic; platforms other than Turnitin and Canvas.

Known limits: it works in Chrome or Edge only; it reads typed Word and PDF files, not scans or handwriting; one educator per piece of marking or moderation; marks are interpreted on the UK university marking scale.

Get started

Try it first with the made-up coursework.

Feedbacker comes with made-up coursework to practise on. For now, setting it up takes a few technical steps: you need Chrome or Edge, Node.js 24 or later, a key for Anthropic's AI (paid for by use), and to type a few commands. Get started walks you through it; the user guide then takes you through marking a cohort, or a moderation, step by step.
