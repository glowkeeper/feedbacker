Marking, feedback and moderation · Open source

Assessment needs more than an answer.

Feedbacker helps educators in higher education use AI to help them mark and moderate coursework against a rubric and brief. The AI suggests; you decide every mark, and approve every word a student receives.

Built, and used for real on the maintainer's own marking and moderation.

What it does

Two workflows. Your judgement in both.

Marking and feedback

  • Import a whole cohort from Turnitin's or Canvas's bulk download.
  • Mark against the rubric, seeing the AI's proposed levels first, or only after you have marked (blind).
  • The AI drafts feedback from your marks and comments, and Feedbacker checks it against them: praise above the mark, a missing next step, another mark named.
  • Compare the cohort's feedback side by side, approve exactly what each student receives, and paste or export it.

Moderation

  • Import the sample of already-marked work, and the original marking.
  • Re-mark it against the rubric, openly or blind, with an independent AI second reading beside the original marker's.
  • See where you, the marker and the AI differ, criterion by criterion, and record your verdicts.
  • Approve and export the moderation record, ready for the moderation form.

What it never does

AI assists.
Educators decide.

01

It never gives a mark

The AI proposes levels, with its reasons and quoted evidence. Any provisional mark is worked out in code, shown apart from yours, and never is yours.

02

Nothing released without you

You approve exactly what each student receives, and any later change clears that approval. Feedbacker never sends anything to students or to your institution's systems.

03

Every AI contribution traceable

Each proposal, draft and suggestion records the AI, its instructions and what it was sent, and feedback adapted from one is marked as such.

04

Accessible by design

Every screen is measured against WCAG 2.2 AA checks, every control can be reached from the keyboard, and it has been used with a screen reader.

Your data

It stays with you, unless you approve it.

Feedbacker is not a hosted service: there are no accounts and no Feedbacker server. It runs on your computer, and your work lives in folders you keep.

Names, IDs and contact details are replaced on your computer before anything can be sent. You review each anonymised text, and each figure, and approve it. Only then, and only when you confirm it, is it sent to the AI (Anthropic's Claude, with your own API key), with Feedbacker's instructions and the rubric; and, when the AI drafts feedback, your own anonymised marks and comments for that one student. It goes through a small program on your computer that records what it sends, without the text.

Anonymisation is rule-based and can miss things, and it can't see inside images: your review is the safeguard.

Where it stands

Early, honest, and evaluated.

Both workflows are built, and have been used for real on the maintainer's own marking and moderation: read the evaluations of moderation and of marking and feedback.

Not yet: an installer; team marking and calibration across markers; other AI providers; other marking platforms.

Known limits: Chrome or Edge only; Turnitin and Canvas downloads; typed .docx and .pdf work (no OCR); one educator per workspace; marks read against the UK higher-education scale.

Get started

Try it with synthetic coursework first.

For now, setting it up takes a few technical steps: Chrome or Edge, Node.js, an Anthropic API key and a terminal. The README walks you through it, and the runbook takes you through marking a cohort, or a moderation, step by step.
